# RULES.md

## 1. Keep It Simple

* Build only what the current iteration requires.
* Prefer simple functions and data structures.
* Do not add abstractions for possible future needs.
* Do not add frameworks unless they are clearly necessary.

## 2. Work Iteratively

* Complete one iteration at a time.
* Keep the application working after every iteration.
* Do not implement future iterations early.
* Do not mix unrelated changes into the current iteration.

## 3. Keep It Understandable

* The project owner must be able to explain the complete execution flow.
* Use clear names and direct control flow.
* Avoid hidden behavior and unnecessary indirection.
* Simplify the code whenever it becomes difficult to follow.

## 5. Analyze the Reason for the Decline

* A large price decline alone must not be treated as an investment opportunity.
* Identify the likely reasons for the decline before evaluating the company.
* Consider company-specific events, financial performance, industry conditions, and broader market movements.
* Distinguish temporary market reactions from deterioration in the underlying business.

## 6. Evaluate Both Opportunity and Risk

* Every analysis must consider both the potential investment case and the reasons the investment may fail.
* Prioritize the risk of permanent capital loss over short-term price volatility.
* Consider financial strength, growth, profitability, valuation, competition, management, and business durability.
* Do not assume that a stock will return to its previous price.

## 7. Make the Analysis Transparent

* Clearly separate facts, calculations, assumptions, and conclusions.
* Explain the evidence supporting each important conclusion.
* Present evidence that weakens or contradicts the investment thesis.
* Avoid unexplained scores, rankings, or recommendations.
* State uncertainty when the available evidence does not support a confident conclusion.

## 10. Test Important Financial Logic

* Test calculations, filters, classifications, and decision rules.
* Use deterministic data for automated tests.
* Do not weaken tests merely to make them pass.
